# DS_Zipline V0.3.5 — two-tier movement spike

This is a deliberately limited runtime prototype for 7 Days to Die V3.2.0 b9.
It tests whether a local player can move smoothly along a persistent vanilla
wire while retaining a stable camera and normal multiplayer position sync.

## Prototype controls

1. Craft and place two matching anchors with at least 2 m of vertical drop:
   - **Wooden Zipline Anchors** are hand-crafted from common wood, scrap iron,
     and leather. Their detailed survivor-built timber/rope/iron model uses a
     static black cable and travels at 4 m/s; no progression unlock is required.
   - **Sonic Zipline Anchors** require a workbench, forged steel/end-game parts,
     and the Electrician tier-4 (car-battery) unlock. They retain the red/black
     source model, continuous cyan emission cable, and travel at 16 m/s.
   Both anchor models use dirt, grit, and oxidized weathering rather than clean
   flat colors, and both recipes appear in the Building crafting category.
   Each post reserves a native 1×3×1 vertical footprint; keep all three cells clear.
2. Craft and equip the **Zipline Tool**. It is a low-cost hand recipe that
   unlocks at the same progression tier as the regular wrench.
3. Use its secondary action on one anchor and then the other. Tiers cannot be mixed.
4. Interact with the higher anchor and choose **Ride Zipline**. The camera and
   character snap to the downhill cable tangent at launch; camera look remains free.
   A custom red/black trolley follows the exact procedural cable curve. The raised
   cable and procedural hand IK place the rider's hands near the trolley instead of
   passing the cable through the face. The airborne falling animation is suppressed
   while attached. IK constraints are explicitly restored on every detach path, and
   reused IK controllers are rebuilt so switching tiers retains the hand grip.
5. Press Jump or Use to release early.

Wooden connections may be up to 250 m and Sonic connections up to 500 m. Long
routes use a dedicated scalable cable mesh and the server's persistent power graph
because vanilla electrical wires stop at 256 m and require both chunks to be loaded.

## Expected limitations

- Fixed tier speeds (wood 4 m/s, Sonic 16 m/s); no gravity acceleration yet.
- No collision sweep or safe landing selection yet.
- No server ride-authorization package yet.
- The Zipline Tool rejects ordinary powered blocks and mixed-tier links on the
  client, with matching authoritative dedicated-server validation.
- One route per test anchor is recommended.
- Remote-player pose is not polished.
- The Zipline Tool uses a custom CC BY bolt-cutter model and generated icon. Its
  compact 0.55 m size and first-/third-person grip transforms are independently tuned.
- The source scene's fixed 12.9 m cable is retained as a reference asset only;
  gameplay retains the variable-length sagging cable generated by the game.
- Custom post/trolley art is constructed from a client-side runtime mesh payload;
  no Unity AssetBundle is loaded by the game. Both anchor inventory icons are
  generated directly from their corresponding normalized models.
- The three multiblock cells and ModelEntity root collider use tier-specific bounds.
  Activating any visible section targets the powered parent.
- Vanilla chunk rendering toggles are patched because they blindly enable every
  renderer under the fallback prefab; this prevents nearby door animation/occlusion
  changes from revealing the electric-fence model.

Runtime tests should use one dedicated test instance and save. The remaining
observations are trolley alignment, camera obstruction, controller jitter,
rubber-banding, remote-player smoothing, and safe restoration of normal walking
after arrival or release.
